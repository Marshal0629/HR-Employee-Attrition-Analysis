# Business Insights & HR Recommendations

## Executive Summary

The HR Employee Attrition analysis was conducted to identify employee groups and workplace factors associated with higher employee turnover.

The dataset contains 1,470 employee records, of which 237 employees had left the organization, resulting in an overall observed attrition rate of 16.12%.

The analysis identified several notable patterns. Attrition was particularly high among employees working overtime, employees in their early tenure, employees in the lowest income group, and employees in certain job roles. Interaction analysis further showed that specific combinations of these factors were associated with substantially higher observed attrition.

The strongest observed risk segments included employees in the lowest income group who worked overtime, with an attrition rate of 58.49%, and employees with 0–2 years at the company who worked overtime, with an attrition rate of 50.96%.

These findings suggest that employee retention efforts should focus not only on individual factors but also on identifying employee groups experiencing combinations of workload, compensation, early tenure, and role-related pressures.

The analysis provides evidence-based insights that can help HR prioritize retention initiatives, while recognizing that the observed relationships represent associations and do not establish causal relationships.

## Key Business Insights

### Insight 1 — Overtime is a Major Attrition Signal

Employees who worked overtime had an observed attrition rate of 30.53%, compared with 10.44% among employees who did not work overtime.

This means the observed attrition rate among overtime employees was approximately 2.9 times higher than among non-overtime employees.

**Business implication:**  
Employees experiencing sustained overtime may represent an important retention-risk group. HR should examine workload distribution, staffing levels, and recurring overtime patterns across teams.

**Priority:** High

---

### Insight 2 — Early-Tenure Employees Face Higher Attrition

Employees with 0–2 years at the company had an observed attrition rate of 29.82%, the highest among the tenure groups analyzed.

Attrition generally decreased as employees accumulated more years at the company.

**Business implication:**  
The first few years of employment appear to be a critical retention period. HR may benefit from strengthening onboarding, mentoring, early-career support, and regular employee check-ins during this period.

**Priority:** High

---

### Insight 3 — Early Tenure Combined with Overtime Creates a High-Risk Segment

The interaction analysis showed that employees with 0–2 years at the company who worked overtime had an observed attrition rate of 50.96%, compared with 20.59% among employees with 0–2 years who did not work overtime.

**Business implication:**  
Early-tenure employees who are regularly exposed to overtime may require targeted retention attention. HR could monitor overtime among newer employees and investigate whether workload, staffing, or onboarding support is contributing to the observed risk.

**Priority:** Very High

---

### Insight 4 — Lower Income Combined with Overtime Shows the Highest Observed Attrition Among Key Risk Segments

Employees in the Q1 – Lowest income group who worked overtime had an observed attrition rate of 58.49%, compared with 17.49% among employees in the same income group who did not work overtime.

Among overtime employees, observed attrition decreased as income increased.

**Business implication:**  
The combination of lower compensation and overtime represents a particularly important retention-risk segment. HR could review workload, staffing, compensation competitiveness, and retention measures for employees in lower income groups who regularly work overtime.

**Priority:** Very High

---

### Insight 5 — Attrition Varies Considerably by Job Role

Sales Representatives had the highest overall observed attrition rate at 39.76%. Laboratory Technicians and Human Resources employees also showed relatively high attrition rates.

The interaction analysis showed that overtime was associated with particularly high attrition in some roles. Sales Representatives working overtime had an observed attrition rate of 66.67%, while Laboratory Technicians working overtime had an observed attrition rate of 50.00%. The Sales Representative + Overtime subgroup contains 24 employees, so its 66.67% attrition rate should be interpreted as a potential risk signal rather than a definitive estimate.

**Business implication:**  
Retention strategies may need to be tailored by job role rather than applying the same intervention across the entire workforce. Roles with high attrition and high overtime exposure should receive particular attention.

**Priority:** High

---

### Insight 6 — Frequent Business Travel is Associated with Higher Attrition

Employees who travelled frequently had an observed attrition rate of 24.91%, compared with 8.00% among employees who did not travel.

**Business implication:**  
Frequent travel may represent an additional retention concern. HR could review travel frequency, employee workload, travel-related support, and work-life balance among frequently travelling employees.

**Priority:** Medium-High

## HR Recommendations

### Recommendation 1 — Reduce and Monitor Excessive Overtime

**Related insight:** Overtime is associated with substantially higher employee attrition.

**Recommended action:**
- Monitor overtime hours by department, team, and job role.
- Identify employees and teams with consistently high overtime.
- Review workload distribution and staffing levels.
- Investigate whether recurring overtime is caused by understaffing, unrealistic workloads, or operational requirements.
- Introduce workload-balancing measures where appropriate.

**Expected outcome:**  
Reducing sustained excessive overtime may improve employee work-life balance and help reduce retention risk.

---

### Recommendation 2 — Strengthen Early-Tenure Retention Programs

**Related insight:** Employees with 0–2 years at the company have the highest overall observed attrition.

**Recommended action:**
- Strengthen onboarding and orientation programs.
- Introduce structured mentoring or buddy programs for new employees.
- Conduct regular check-ins during the first two years.
- Provide clearer career-development and progression information.
- Identify early signs of disengagement before employees decide to leave.

**Expected outcome:**  
Better support during the early employment period may improve employee integration, engagement, and retention.

---

### Recommendation 3 — Prioritize Early-Tenure Employees Working Overtime

**Related insight:** Employees with 0–2 years at the company who work overtime have an observed attrition rate of 50.96%.

**Recommended action:**
- Create an early-tenure overtime monitoring group.
- Review overtime frequency among employees in their first two years.
- Conduct targeted manager or HR check-ins.
- Evaluate workload and staffing requirements.
- Provide additional support or mentoring where necessary.

**Expected outcome:**  
Targeting this specific high-risk segment allows HR to focus retention resources where the observed attrition risk is particularly high.

---

### Recommendation 4 — Review Compensation and Workload for Lower-Income Overtime Employees

**Related insight:** Employees in the lowest income group who work overtime have an observed attrition rate of 58.49%.

**Recommended action:**
- Identify lower-income employees with recurring overtime.
- Review whether compensation remains competitive for the responsibilities and workload involved.
- Examine workload and staffing conditions in teams with high overtime.
- Consider targeted compensation reviews or retention incentives where appropriate.
- Track attrition within this employee segment over time.

**Expected outcome:**  
Addressing both workload and compensation concerns may help HR reduce retention risk among this particularly vulnerable employee segment.

---

### Recommendation 5 — Develop Role-Specific Retention Strategies

**Related insight:** Attrition varies considerably across job roles, with Sales Representatives showing the highest overall observed attrition.

**Recommended action:**
- Analyze turnover patterns separately by job role.
- Investigate workload, career progression, compensation, and management conditions within high-attrition roles.
- Pay particular attention to roles with both high attrition and high overtime exposure.
- Develop role-specific retention initiatives rather than applying identical interventions across all employees.

**Expected outcome:**  
Role-specific interventions can address the different working conditions and retention challenges experienced by different employee groups.

---

### Recommendation 6 — Review the Impact of Frequent Business Travel

**Related insight:** Employees who travel frequently have substantially higher observed attrition than non-travelling employees.

**Recommended action:**
- Monitor employees with frequent travel requirements.
- Review travel frequency and workload.
- Assess whether travel schedules are affecting work-life balance.
- Provide appropriate travel-related support and recovery time where feasible.
- Consider whether travel requirements can be distributed more evenly across employees.

**Expected outcome:**  
Better management of travel demands may improve employee well-being and reduce retention risk among frequently travelling employees.

---

## Recommendation Prioritization

| Priority | Recommendation | Reason |

| **Very High** | Support early-tenure employees working overtime | 50.96% observed attrition |
| **High** | Reduce excessive overtime | 30.53% overall attrition among overtime employees |
| **High** | Strengthen early-tenure retention | 29.82% attrition among employees with 0–2 years |
| **High** | Develop role-specific retention strategies | Large variation across job roles |

### Important Consideration

These recommendations are based on observed associations in the available employee data. They should be treated as areas for HR investigation and targeted intervention rather than proof that a particular factor directly causes employee attrition.

## Priority Action Plan

Based on the observed attrition patterns, HR can prioritize retention efforts in the following order:

### Priority 1 — Address High-Risk Workload and Compensation Segments

Focus on employees who combine **lower income with regular overtime**, as this group recorded the highest substantial observed attrition rate of 58.49%.

**Action:** Review workload, staffing, overtime frequency, and compensation competitiveness for this segment.

### Priority 2 — Protect Early-Tenure Employees

Employees with 0–2 years at the company showed elevated attrition, particularly when combined with overtime.

**Action:** Strengthen onboarding, mentoring, early-career support, and regular HR check-ins while monitoring overtime during the first two years.

### Priority 3 — Target High-Attrition Job Roles

Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians showed particularly high attrition, with overtime further increasing observed attrition within these roles.

**Action:** Conduct role-specific reviews of workload, career progression, compensation, management support, and working conditions.

### Priority 4 — Review Frequent Business Travel

Employees who travelled frequently showed substantially higher attrition than non-travelling employees.

**Action:** Review travel frequency, workload, scheduling, and employee support for frequently travelling employees.

## Expected Business Impact

If the recommended actions are implemented and monitored effectively, the organization could potentially:

- Reduce excessive overtime and workload-related retention risks.
- Improve retention during the critical early-tenure period.
- Identify high-risk employee segments earlier.
- Improve workload and staffing decisions using employee data.
- Develop more targeted retention strategies for high-attrition roles.
- Improve employee experience and work-life balance.
- Support more data-driven HR decision-making.

The effectiveness of these interventions should be evaluated by tracking attrition rates over time across the targeted employee segments.
